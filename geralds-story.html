Gerald's Story

From apprentice to Managing Director — the man who shaped P&A's journey from workshop to world-class fabricator.

His Journey

Apprentice to Director

Gerald Anthony's story at P&A began as an apprentice on the shop floor — learning to cut, bend, and weld steel with the same hands-on rigour he would later demand of every new employee. The trade got into him early: the smell of hot metal, the sound of a press brake, the satisfaction of a tight tolerance met.

Through dedication and a relentless attention to quality, Gerald rose through every level of the business. He understood fabrication not just as a product — but as a system of decisions, each one building on the last to determine whether a component would meet its specification or not.

When Gerald became Managing Director, he brought that shop-floor mentality to business strategy. His first priority was formalising what P&A already did well — through the framework of ISO 9001 quality management certification. "Quality is not something you inspect in," he would say. "It is something you design in." The ISO certification forced P&A to document, measure, and continuously improve every process in the business.

But Gerald's most audacious move came in 2007, when he led P&A to become the first company in Africa to implement MIE Trak — an advanced electronic manufacturing tracking system developed in the United States. At the time, the concept of real-time production visibility was largely foreign to South African fabricators. Gerald saw it differently: if you cannot see exactly where every job is at every moment, you cannot truly control quality or delivery.

The MIE Trak implementation transformed P&A's capacity to serve large, demanding clients who needed not just product — but certainty. Every job is now logged from the moment an enquiry arrives, through engineering, production, quality inspection, and delivery. Clients can trust that their component will arrive on time, to specification, every time.

Beyond systems, Gerald's deepest conviction has always been about people. Watching young South Africans struggle to enter the manufacturing sector — and watching the sector itself struggle to find skilled workers — he drove the creation of the P&A Sheet Metal Training Academy. The Academy became the first QCTO and MERSETA accredited sheet metal training facility in the country, providing a pathway into a skilled trade for dozens of young people from KwaZulu-Natal.

"If we do not invest in skills, we have no manufacturing industry," Gerald has said. "And without a manufacturing industry, South Africa has no future." The Academy represents that belief, made real.

Today, Gerald leads a company that his apprentice self would barely recognise — but one that holds every value he learned on the floor: precision, commitment, and the belief that doing it right matters.
