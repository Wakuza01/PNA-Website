The Importance of Safety in a Growing Company

Home / News & Insights / Safety

Growth creates pressure — on capacity, on scheduling, on people. One of the most common failure modes in a growing manufacturing company is allowing safety standards to slip as throughput demands increase. At P&A Fabricators, this is a line we're not willing to cross.

Sheet metal fabrication is an inherently hazardous environment. High-powered laser cutters, CNC press brakes generating tonnes of clamping force, welding arcs, grinding sparks, and heavy material handling are daily realities. Managing these hazards isn't optional — it's a legal, ethical, and operational requirement.

The OHS Act Framework

P&A operates in full compliance with South Africa's Occupational Health and Safety Act (OHSA) 85 of 1993 and its associated regulations. This means documented risk assessments for each work area, a designated safety officer, regular safety inspections, and formal incident reporting procedures.

Compliance with the OHS Act isn't simply about avoiding penalties. It's about creating a documented system that identifies hazards before they become incidents — and ensures that when near-misses occur, they're recorded and analysed rather than ignored.

PPE and Machine Guarding

All production operators at P&A are issued with appropriate personal protective equipment for their work area — including cut-resistant gloves, safety spectacles, face shields where required, steel-capped footwear, and welding PPE (helmets, aprons, gauntlets) for the welding bay.

Machine guarding on laser cutters and press brakes is maintained to OEM specification. Safety interlocks are tested as part of the regular maintenance schedule and any faults are addressed before the machine is returned to production.

Safety as Culture

Procedures and PPE matter. But they're not enough on their own. A safe workshop requires a culture where operators feel empowered to stop work when something is wrong, where supervisors don't pressure teams to bypass safety steps under deadline pressure, and where near-miss reporting is treated as valuable data rather than a disciplinary trigger.

P&A management is committed to maintaining this culture as the company grows. New team members receive safety induction before they set foot on the production floor, and safety is a standing agenda item at team meetings.
