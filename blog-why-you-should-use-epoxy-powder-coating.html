Why You Should Use Epoxy Powder Coating

Home / News & Insights / Technical Articles

If you're specifying a finish for fabricated steel components, the choice between wet paint and epoxy powder coating is rarely close. For most industrial and engineering applications, powder coating offers a demonstrably superior result — in adhesion, corrosion resistance, coating consistency, and long-term durability.

Here's what engineers and designers need to understand about powder coating and why it's become the default specification for precision fabrications across South African industry.

What Is Epoxy Powder Coating?

Powder coating is a dry finishing process. Electrostatically charged powder particles — in this case, an epoxy-based formulation — are sprayed onto the prepared metal surface and adhere via electrostatic attraction. The coated component is then cured in an oven, typically at 180–200°C, where the powder melts, flows, and cross-links into a hard, continuous film.

The epoxy chemistry gives the coating strong adhesion to metal substrates, excellent chemical resistance, and high mechanical hardness — making it particularly well-suited to components that will be exposed to oils, cleaning agents, or physical abrasion in service.

Corrosion Resistance

For mild steel fabrications destined for industrial environments, corrosion protection is a primary design concern. Powder coating — when applied over a correctly prepared surface — provides a continuous, pinhole-free barrier that significantly outperforms equivalent-thickness wet paint systems in salt spray testing (ASTM B117).

Surface preparation is critical. At P&A, components are cleaned, degreased, and shot-blasted or chemically pre-treated before coating. A coating applied over a poorly prepared surface will fail regardless of its inherent properties.

Coating Consistency

One of wet paint's limitations in a production environment is film thickness variability. Skilled operators reduce this, but inconsistency remains. Powder coating applied electrostatically self-levels to a greater degree and wraps around edges and into recesses more effectively than wet spray application, producing more consistent dry film thickness (DFT) across complex geometries.

DFT is typically measured and recorded as part of our quality process. Standard specification is 60–80 microns, with tight-tolerance requirements confirmed at quoting stage.

Environmental and Operational Advantages

Powder coating contains no solvents — it releases no volatile organic compounds (VOCs) during application or curing. Overspray is recoverable and recyclable. For clients with environmental management systems (ISO 14001 or similar), powder coating is a cleaner option than solvent-based wet paint.

Because the process is in-house at P&A, components don't need to travel to a third-party coating shop. This eliminates a logistics step, reduces handling risk, and keeps lead times shorter.

Specifying Powder Coating

When specifying powder coating on your fabrication drawings, include:

  • Colour reference (RAL or equivalent colour system)
  • Finish type (gloss, semi-gloss, satin, matt, textured)
  • Minimum DFT requirement (if applicable)
  • Any masking requirements (threaded holes, mating faces, etc.)

P&A's team will confirm achievable DFT and any geometry limitations at quoting stage.
